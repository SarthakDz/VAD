# D1 — the claim-selection problem

Level 1 is scored as `25 * F1` over the twenty Level-1 videos, and that single
fact reframes the task. An argmax per video answers *what is this video*. The
metric asks *which claims are worth making*, and those are different questions.

## The operating point was pointed the wrong way

With `F1 = 2f / (n_pred + n_gt)`, adding one more claim of probability `p`
raises the score exactly when

```
p > F1 / 2
```

Adding a claim adds `2p` to the numerator and `1` to the denominator, so it pays
whenever `p` exceeds half the current F1. At the private set's measured F1 of
0.535 that break-even is **p > 0.27** — below the 0.4 threshold the pipeline was
using, and far below the 0.7 that `submission_v5.json` tried.

This explains the v5 result. Raising the threshold from 0.4 to 0.7 dropped four
claims (E001 waterlogging, E004 traffic_accident, E014 wrong_way_driving,
E015 traffic_accident) and D1 fell from 13.4 to 11.4. The threshold was not
merely mistuned; moving it up was moving it the wrong way. There is no fixed
threshold that is right, because the break-even travels with the score.

`scripts/d1_boost.py` implements this as a scan over prefixes of the
probability-sorted claim list, taking whichever prefix maximises expected F1.

## Two sources the pipeline never used

**Retrieval.** A k-NN vote over the 3207-clip train bank (k=5, softmax
temperature 0.05, cosine on mean+max pooled embeddings). Non-parametric, so it
fails differently from anything trained.

**SigLIP's text tower.** `src/encode.py` loads the vision tower alone and says
so — building the tokenizer pulls in SentencePiece "for no benefit". That was
true while nothing used text. But SigLIP has **no projection head**: its
`get_image_features` returns exactly the `pooler_output` already sitting in
`cache/emb/*.npy`. Zero-shot text classification therefore costs one matmul
against a prompt-ensembled text prototype per class, with no re-encoding of a
single frame. Three prompts per class, averaged and renormalised.

On its own the text tower is mediocre (13.2/25 for so400m, 11.8 for base). In
the ensemble it is the difference between 15.3 and 18.1.

## Calibration before averaging

The five sources are averaged, but they are not calibrated to each other — a
trained head that returns 0.97 and a k-NN vote that returns 0.4 are not making
comparably strong statements, and the overconfident member dominates the mean.
Flattening each source with `p^(1/T)` at **T = 2** before averaging fixes this,
and it matters more than which sources are in the mix.

## Results, on the public test set's 24 Level-1 videos

Leakage-free — no collection prior, since that map is built from the public
ground truth being scored against.

| variant | marks | found | false alarms |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| trained base head @0.4 | 11.5 | 9/20 | 10 |
| base + so400m ensemble @0.4 (the recipe that scored 13.4 privately) | 15.3 | 11/20 | 5 |
| k-NN retrieval alone | 13.5 | 10/20 | 7 |
| zero-shot text, so400m, alone | 12.8 | 10/20 | 9 |
| **so400m + k-NN + zero-shot, T=2, F1 selection** | **18.1** | **13/20** | **3** |
| the same plus the base head | 17.6 | 13/20 | 4 |

The gain is recall, not threshold arithmetic: 11 found becomes 13, and false
alarms fall from 5 to 3 at the same time.

It is a plateau rather than a lucky cell. Every combination containing
retrieval, the text tower and at least one trained head scores 16–18 for T
anywhere in [1.5, 3]. Twenty-four videos is a small sample and one video is
worth about 1.4 marks, so the ±1 differences inside that plateau are noise; the
+2.8 over the baseline is larger than the plateau's own spread.

## The collection prior needs a guard

[[fingerprints]] restricts a video to the classes its source collection is known
to contain. Applied to the public set it must be ablated leave-one-out, and
under that ablation it *costs* 3.6 marks. The reason is diagnostic rather than
damning: ablating a public video **deletes the correct class outright** for 5 of
the 20 anomalous ones (T005, T010, T017, T018, T020), every one of them a class
that occurs exactly once in a small group.

That cannot happen on the private set, whose labels were never in the map. But
the failure mode it exposes is real — a collection observed twice cannot
sensibly veto a class. `allowed_guarded()` therefore applies the prior only when
at least three public videos back the collection, exempting normal-only groups
because those can silence a claim but never push one to a wrong class. Thirteen
of the twenty private Level-1 videos get a mask under that rule; E003, E004,
E005, E007, E008, E017 and E018 belong to collections with no public twin and
are left to the model.

## What did not work

**Motion features.** The four classes the arena reports as never found —
`fighting_or_violence` (3 truths), `road_spill_or_debris` (3),
`stalled_or_broken_down_vehicle` (2), `vehicle_blocking_traffic` (1) — are
exactly the four the public set also fails, and every one of them is a confusion
with a dynamics opposite: fighting mistaken for loitering, a spill for an
accident, a stalled vehicle for wrong-way driving. That suggests frame-to-frame
embedding distance should separate them for free.

It does not. In the `(896,448,1.88)` collection the two loitering videos have
motion 0.024 and 0.059 while the two fighting videos have 0.022 and 0.047 — the
*highest*-motion video in the group is loitering. The hypothesis is clean, cheap
and wrong, and one measurement killed it.

**Fixed thresholds below 0.4.** Lowering the threshold helps the uncalibrated
ensembles a little (ens4 rises from 13.6 at 0.4 to 16.2 at 0.25) but the
F1-optimal rule dominates it once the sources are flattened, and needs no
threshold at all.

## Private predictions

Twelve claims of twenty, from `ens3 T2 so+knn+zs` with the guarded prior. The
cut is insensitive to the assumed anomaly count: twelve claims for any `n_gt`
between 9 and 13, thirteen at 15 and above.

```
E001 waterlogging_or_flood   E009 smoke                  E016 traffic_accident
E006 traffic_congestion      E010 smoke                  E019 loitering
E012 waterlogging_or_flood   E011 smoke                  E020 loitering
E013 waterlogging_or_flood   E014 wrong_way_driving      E015 traffic_accident
```

E002, E003, E004, E005, E007, E008, E017, E018 answer normal.

Two caveats worth carrying. Three `smoke` claims and no `fire` in the
`(640,640,24.0)` collection is suspicious — the public half of that collection
splits two fire and two smoke, and fire↔smoke is this model's oldest documented
confusion. And E019 and E020 are both called loitering at p≈0.97 in a collection
the public set splits evenly between fighting and loitering; the classifier has
no discriminative power there at all, calling all four public videos loitering,
so one of the two is likely wrong. Swapping one to fighting does not change the
expected score — one right either way — so it is left alone.
